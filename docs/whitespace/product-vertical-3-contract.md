# Third product vertical: exploratory unobserved pairs (contract, draft)

**Status:** Contract approved by the review of #177 (merged 2026-10-26). **Amendment §6.1 (integrity at load), with the tests and scenario M8 it adds in §8, accepted in pull request #185 (merged 2026-10-27).** **A second amendment is proposed in the pull request that edits this line and is not yet merged:** the wording on presentation arithmetic in §3, the evidence sheet in §5 and the data in §6 (D1–D3 of the E5 evidence specification); until it is merged, those parts are a proposal. **The view is not implemented**, and the Spanish terms of §3 and §4 remain proposals until approved. Neither the approval of the contract nor the acceptance of the amendment authorises exposing the view: exposure requires the gates of §7, §8 and §9, among them the manual scenarios M1–M7 and, once the amendment is accepted, M8 (ADR 0038 is not modified: it names M1–M7, and the contract's own gate adds M8). The acceptance scenarios below are new and proposed; none comes from an earlier decision.
**Date:** 2026-10-26
**Checks:** [ADR 0038](../adr/0038-exploratory-white-space-product-track.md) (E1–E8; Proposed in #176, Accepted by the pull request that follows #177) · [ADR 0036](../adr/0036-technology-white-space-scientific-scope.md) (§5.4, §9, C3) · [vertical 1 contract](product-vertical-1-contract.md) · [vertical 2 contract](product-vertical-2-contract.md)
**Scope:** A view in which the user explores pairs of IPC subclasses **not observed together** in the OEPM corpus up to 2025, with the expected count under independence beside each. **Exploratory and not validated: no prediction, no probability, no score, no recommendation.** It does not change the explorer of vertical 2, which stays descriptive.

## 1. Relation to the ADR

ADR 0038 allows this view under rules E1–E8. This contract turns those rules into requirements that can be checked. Where the ADR leaves a reading open, the contract says so in §9 instead of deciding it silently. ADR 0038 is *Accepted* by the pull request that follows #177. Code is written only as this contract describes it, in small pull requests; exposing the view needs the gates of §7, §8 and §9.

## 2. What the user can do

1. Open the view from an anchor of the front page (no router, no new dependency). The corpus period is fixed: documents up to 2025, with the 2010 gap stated.
2. Read the notice of §4 before the list.
3. Search a subclass by symbol, or choose a section. **No list of pairs loads before one of the two is chosen** (decision of the review, §9.3). The initial state explains how to start: choose a subclass or a section; it shows no pair and no count of pairs presented as a result.
4. Filter: section; **type A** (every unobserved pair of two active subclasses) or **type A with expected count ≥ 1** (ADR 0036 type B, *e*_min = 1, fixed).
5. Sort. Default **alphabetical by symbol of the first, then the second subclass**. The user may choose to sort by expected count (descending), by documents of either subclass, or by symbol. The first choice of the expected-count sort shows the notice of §4.
6. Open a pair: the evidence of §5.

The user cannot: change *m*_min or *e*_min, mark pairs as good or bad, export a ranked list, or share a link that carries a sort by expected count as if it were a result (the link keeps the default order).

## 3. Rules

* **Universe.** Active subclasses at 2025 (≥ 20 publication identifiers, O4) and the pairs among them. The pair is *not observed* if no single source record published up to 2025 carries both.
* **Expected count.** Exactly the quantity and formula of ADR 0038 E3, computed offline and read as data. The browser filters and sorts the stored value and **computes no metric**: nothing it computes is used to list, filter, sort, label or score a pair. The one exception is **presentation arithmetic** on the evidence sheet (§5): reconstructing, from the verified per-year counts of the result, the per-year terms of the formula already defined and their sum, to compare it with the stored value. It is not a general authorisation to compute anything else in the browser. Years not observed (2010) are skipped, never zero. Shown with one decimal in the list; the unrounded value stays in the data.
* **Names (Spanish).** Pair type A: «par no observado en este corpus y periodo». Type A with expected ≥ 1: «par no observado con esperado ≥ 1 bajo independencia». Column: «Esperado bajo independencia (coincidencias)». These are the only labels. No other quality or relevance label exists.
* **Fixed values.** *m*_min = 20 and *e*_min = 1 are stated in the limits block beside every list (the text of §4, «Parameters»), as fixed by design and not tuned to the results.
* **Limits beside the list, not on another page:** kind `Y` absent in 2000–2009, the 2010 gap, the 2023 drop of EP translations, and the remaining limits of C3. Whether 2025 is complete is checked at implementation and stated (§9).
* **Rights.** Source attribution and the download date as in vertical 1. The view is served only inside the tailnet.
* Only symbols are shown; no IPC titles (as in vertical 2).
* Language: Spanish.

## 4. Mandatory texts

* **Notice above every list (E6):** «Exploratorio. Sin validar. Ningún orden mostrado aquí es una predicción.» Followed by one sentence that the study has not shown that any ordering predicts anything, with a link to ADR 0036.
* **Notice at the first expected-count sort:** «Orden por esperado bajo independencia: refleja cuántas coincidencias se esperarían si las dos subclases fueran independientes; no mide probabilidad de aparición ni interés.»
* **Parameters (in the limits block beside every list):** «Parámetros fijados por diseño, no ajustados a los resultados: una subclase es activa con al menos 20 identificadores de publicación; el esperado mínimo para el tipo con esperado ≥ 1 es 1.»
* **Absence sentence of vertical 1** wherever unobserved pairs are listed.
* **Study status line** of vertical 1 stays on the page; if the study has a negative or unresolved result, it is shown here too (E7).

### Forbidden formulations (tested negatively)

None of these, nor their translations or obvious variants, may appear in the page text, in labels, or in accessible names, **except inside the exact sentences of the allowlist below**: *oportunidad, hueco prometedor, potencial, atractivo, recomendado, mejor, top, ranking, puntuación, probabilidad, p-valor, significativo, white space* (as a result label), *innovación* as an outcome, and the closed set of money and market words: *euro, €, dólar, $, precio, mercado, ventas, ingresos, rentabilidad, inversión, negocio, comercial*. **Scope of the checks:** the automated test covers exactly the words listed here, detected as **whole terms** (word boundaries, never arbitrary substrings, so a legitimate word that contains a forbidden sequence by accident is not rejected), case-insensitively and without accents, in singular and plural (the symbols € and $ are matched as characters), over the page text, labels, accessible names and data labels, and the expressions «white space» and «top N» in any case. Synonyms and variants that are not in this list are covered only by the human reading of M3, and the contract does not claim otherwise. **Allowlist (negations that delimit what a quantity is not).** The forbidden words may appear only inside these sentences, verbatim: the notice at the first expected-count sort (it says the quantity does not measure probability), the absence sentence of vertical 1 (it denies an opportunity), and the study status line of vertical 1. The list is closed: a sentence is added only by amending this contract, and each allowlisted sentence must be a negation or a limit, never an affirmation. The negative tests (§8) fail on any occurrence of a forbidden word outside those strings, which are read from the same constants the page renders, so the test and the page cannot drift apart. That each allowlisted sentence is a negation is a human check (scenario M3), because a test cannot read meaning.

Also forbidden: a numeric rank column, a position badge, a colour scale that encodes the expected count as better or worse, and any element that shows only the first *N* sorted pairs as a headline.

## 5. Evidence of a pair (E5)

Opening a pair shows (the rules for the per-year terms follow this paragraph): both symbols; documents per subclass up to 2025 and by publication year; documents with both (0 by definition); the expected count and its terms (the per-year contributions); the corpus identity, the SHA-256 of the result and the configuration identifier; with the completeness comparison of the limits, the name and SHA-256 of the OEPM inventory (Authority File), **taken from the exposure result and labelled as such**, not from this result; the limits of §3; and the activity evidence of each subclass. The activity evidence of each subclass is the **full list** of its publication identifiers with dates, loaded on demand when the evidence is opened (§9.1); this is the literal reading of ADR 0038 E5.

**Rules of the per-year terms (presentation arithmetic).**

1. The stored expected count remains the reference for listing, filtering and sorting, and is the value the sheet presents as the pair's expected count.
2. The sheet shows the input counts n_i(y) and n_j(y), N(y), the term n_i(y)·n_j(y)/N(y) of **each observed year**, and the reconstructed sum next to the stored value.
3. Years not observed (2010 included) are omitted, never shown with a count of zero.
4. **One criterion, no tolerance.** Each term is the product of the two integer counts divided by N(y); the terms are summed in ascending order of year; the sum is rounded to the nine stored decimals and compared with the stored value. If the two are equal the pair is concordant. Otherwise the sheet **shows a discrepancy**: the stored value is never corrected or replaced, and the sheet does not present the pair as consistent. There is no additional margin: a difference of one unit in the last decimal is a discrepancy.
5. Nothing computed on the sheet feeds a filter, a sort, a new metric or any label. No score of opportunity exists.
6. The invariant «reconstructed sum agrees with the stored value» is verified for **every unobserved pair** of the result by the pull request that produces the data, not by a sample.

## 6. Data

* A new versioned result produced offline by a script from the corpus, in its own module, reusing the unit rules of the study (O4) but **not importing study detector or baseline code and not named F1, null model or baseline**.
* Per active subclass: documents by year (observed years only), stored in the result as `documents_by_year`, and a separate static file with all its publication identifiers and dates, fetched only when the user opens the evidence. Per unobserved pair: the expected count. The corpus identity hashes are recorded in the result. The result reads only the bibliographic files, so it records no Authority File hash; the identity of the Authority File is recorded in the exposure result and shown with the completeness comparison (§5).
* **Configuration identifier.** The result records `configuration: { id, parameters }`. `parameters` has exactly: `schema` (`vertical3-unobserved-pairs/1`), `origin`, `min_documents`, `e_min`, `unit` (`O4`: the publication identifier, with the union of the subclasses of its earliest date) and `quantity` (`ADR 0038 E3`); each of them changes what the generation computes or how the result is read. The number of stored decimals and any display format are **not** parameters: they are a storage precision and a presentation, documented separately. `id` is the SHA-256 of the canonical form: a JSON object with its keys sorted by code point, no whitespace, UTF-8, integers written as integers and every other number written as a decimal **string** without trailing zeros (so `e_min` is `"1"`), so that the same content gives the same bytes in any environment. For the current configuration the canonical form is `{"e_min":"1","min_documents":20,"origin":2025,"quantity":"ADR 0038 E3","schema":"vertical3-unobserved-pairs/1","unit":"O4"}` and its SHA-256 is `456858bd653d792244206f9ef5a531524279c4f5d2402b1f016ebf435f883bd8` (a test vector for the pull request that implements it; a change of the parameter set changes it).
* **What the identifier does and does not identify.** It identifies the parameters. It does not identify the code that generated the result: for that, the SHA-256 of the result, the schema version and the tests that regenerate and compare the result are complementary evidence, not an unambiguous identification of the code executed. The sheet and the texts must not attribute to the identifier more than this.
* Size is measured at implementation and stated. The result is loaded on demand as a static file (as the 6.2 MB pairs file), without an endpoint, unless the measurement says otherwise.
* The corpus stays out of git; only the derived result is versioned.
* Invariants tested: the number of active subclasses and the observed pairs agree with the section-density and subclass-pairs results at the years they share; unobserved + observed = pairs among active; every listed pair is absent from the observed set; the formula on a small hand-computed fixture, with a year missing (it must not count as zero) and with a subclass that has no documents in a year.

### 6.1 Integrity at load (amendment, 2026-10-26)

This section requires, for the vertical 3 data, the verification that the text T19 of the proposed Spanish texts (pull request #184, `docs/whitespace/vertical-3-texts-proposal.md`) presupposes. Without it the hash in `provenance.json` is a control only for the tests, not for what the browser draws.

1. **One loader, and a manifest inside the build.** Every vertical 3 data file the page reads (the unobserved-pairs result and, when they exist, the identifier lists) goes through a single loader. The hashes it compares against are in a manifest that is **part of the page's script bundle**, imported at build time (`provenance.json` for the result), never fetched separately. No ADR defines the manifest of the identifier lists: ADR 0038 E5 asks for the SHA-256 of the corpus files and of the result, not for a manifest. That manifest is defined by the implementation of the lists, as a build-time artifact of the same kind; until it exists no list is loaded.
2. **How the loader reads a file.** A GET with `cache: 'no-store'`. The status must be exactly `200`: a `206` or a `304` is never hashed or used. The bytes are read with `arrayBuffer()`, hashed exactly as `arrayBuffer()` returns them (that is, after whatever HTTP decoding the browser applies, such as `Content-Encoding`; they are not necessarily the bytes stored on the server), compared with the manifest, and **only if they are equal** decoded and parsed with `JSON.parse` and handed to the screen. `response.json()` is never used. Nothing is parsed, drawn or cached for display before the comparison, and no service worker or application cache may serve vertical 3 data. This is a requirement, not something the automated tests prove: it is checked in review by inspecting that the diff registers no service worker and adds no other serving path for these files, and the review records that it was a diff inspection.
3. **Which failure shows which state; in all of them no pair, count or list is drawn and there is no fallback to an older or unverified copy.**
   * A network failure, or an HTTP status other than `200`: the **load-error** state (T18). This includes an exception thrown while reading the body with `arrayBuffer()` because the transfer failed (for example a connection cut mid-body): no bytes were obtained to compare, so it is a load error and nothing is shown. There is no path in which data that was not compared reaches the screen.
   * A body that was received, of any length (short and empty bodies included), whose hash differs from the manifest; a file with no entry in the manifest; an entry without a hash; or a hash that cannot be computed because the implementation fails (for example, out of memory): the **integrity-error** state (T19, once approved).
   * No path skips the comparison.
4. **The hash can be computed in the deployment context.** The Web Crypto digest (`crypto.subtle`) exists only in a secure context (HTTPS or localhost). The staging is plain HTTP on a tailnet address, which is not one, so a loader that relied on it would fail there, and one that skipped the check silently would not verify anything. The loader therefore does not depend on `crypto.subtle`: it uses a SHA-256 implementation shipped with the page without a new dependency (§2, item 1: no new dependency; and §8: the diff adds none), tested against published vectors. If that code is copied or adapted from elsewhere, its origin and its licence are recorded in the repository (in the file header and in the description of the change), and that is checked in review. If `crypto.subtle` is also used, only as an optimisation, it must give identical results on the same inputs and be covered by the same vector tests; there is one set of guarantees, not two. Using HTTPS for the view is an alternative that needs a change in the infrastructure and is not assumed.
5. **What the check protects, and what it does not.** It detects a file that was corrupted, truncated, replaced by another version, or that differs from the manifest of the bundle that is running. It does **not** protect against someone who can alter both the data and the bundle, because the same server serves both, and it does not detect a stale bundle paired with equally stale data. The text of the error must not claim more.
6. **Scope.** This applies to the vertical 3 data only. The data of verticals 1 and 2 are not changed by this amendment.

## 7. Firewall with the study (E7), as requirements

* No file of this vertical imports from, or is imported by, study code; a test fails if a study module path appears in its imports.
* No script or test of this vertical computes precision, AUC-PR, hit rate or any quality of the ordering on any study origin or on outcomes after 2025.
* The access list of the staging view is recorded before anyone is shown the pairs: for each person, whether they may take part in the human review of ADR 0036 §8.11. Anyone who may score does not receive access until the decision is written down.

The documentation gate passes.

Tests added by §6.1:

* **SHA-256 vectors.** The implementation gives the reference result for the empty input, «abc», 55, 56, 64 and 119 bytes of «a», the 56-byte NIST message «abcdbcdecdefdefgefghfghighijhijkijkljklmklmnlmnomnopnopq», and one million «a»; the 55- and 56-byte cases are the last single-block and the first two-block padding. It also gives, for an input of **at least 2.5 MB** (the size of the largest list), the same result as an independent implementation available in the test environment. If `crypto.subtle` is also used, it gives the same results on all of these.
* **Loader, accepted.** A fixture whose manifest hash is right is returned parsed.
* **Loader, rejected, each with the parsed data never reaching the screen:** one changed byte; a truncated body; an empty body; a file absent from the manifest; an entry without a hash; the manifest missing from the bundle (a build that fails because the manifest is absent is a build failure, not a loader test; this test injects an undefined or absent manifest at the loader's entry boundary and checks that it accepts no data). A `206`, a `304`, a `404`, a `500` and a network failure are rejected as load errors. The fetch is called with `cache: 'no-store'`, the response is read with `arrayBuffer()` and `response.json()` is not available to it, and a spy on `JSON.parse` shows it is **not called before the comparison** and not called at all on rejection.
* **Without `crypto.subtle`** (stubbed as undefined): a correct file is **accepted** and the same file with one byte changed is **rejected**, so the test cannot pass with a loader that accepts everything.
* **Screen.** When the loader rejects, the DOM contains **no pair, no count and no list**; a hash mismatch shows the integrity-error text and neither the empty-result text nor the load-error text; a load error does not show the integrity-error text.
* **Distinct states.** Each of the states T15 to T19 and the legitimately empty result renders a different text, and none shares a code path that renders results.
* **The negative tests detect the removal of the check.** Verified once, and recorded in the pull request that implements the loader, by deleting or skipping the comparison and observing that the negative tests above fail.

Each scenario is run by a named person, with date, build identifier and result (pass/fail with notes), in a record committed with the change that exposes the view. A failed scenario blocks exposure.

## 9. Points of the review

Measurements below were made on 2026-10-26 from the local corpus (`BIBLIO_2000…2025`, 711,547 records) and from the versioned exposure result. The first is an approximation by source record, not by the O4 publication identifier, and is to be recomputed at implementation.

1. **Activity evidence (E5). Decided in the review: the full list on demand.** The 20-identifier reading is **withdrawn from the contract**: it was not shown to be equivalent to the ADR, and an interface limit must not relax it. The contract states the literal reading (the full list, on demand). Feasibility, measured: 621 subclasses with at least 20 documents, 1,287,829 subclass-document incidences, about **37 MB** in total as identifier-and-date lists, per subclass a median of about 23 KB, a 95th percentile of about 212 KB and a maximum of about 2.5 MB (`A61K`); loaded one subclass at a time, none of this is on the page's critical path. A shorter list would be an explicit change to E5 by ADR amendment, not a choice of the interface. The final figures are recomputed at implementation under O4.
2. **Completeness of 2025. Criterion and rule on failure adopted; threshold approved as an exposure rule, not as proof of absolute completeness.** The existing exposure result compares the corpus with the OEPM inventory by publication key. At 2025 it records 11 absent of 29,238 inventory keys (EP translations 2 of 23,544, kind `Y` 1 of 1,924, other 8 of 3,770; 0.04 % in total). Criterion, **approved as an operative exposure rule** (not as proof of absolute completeness): before exposure, the exposure result for 2025 must show an absent share of at most **1 % in each kind group**; the figures are quoted in the view's limits. **Rule on failure (decided in the review):** with the measurement available and the criterion met in every group, the view may be exposed, stating that the comparison is against the OEPM inventory. With the criterion not met in any group, or the measurement not available, **exposure of vertical 3 is blocked**: the reason is recorded, the reference year is not changed silently (that is an amendment of ADR 0038), and the view is re-evaluated only after the cause is corrected and the comparison recomputed. A warning on the page never substitutes for this gate. The comparison is against the OEPM's own inventory, not an independent count, and says nothing about records the inventory also lacks.
3. **Size of the global list. Decided in the review: adopted.** The number of unobserved pairs is of the order of 10^5 (measured: 192,510 pairs among 621 active subclasses, 43,462 observed, 149,048 not observed; to be recomputed under O4). The view requires a subclass or a section before any list loads (§2), with the clear initial state above. There is no global list that could read as an editorial selection.
4. **Terms. Proposals, not approved.** The Spanish labels of §3 and the notices of §4 stay proposals until approved, together with the forbidden formulations, because a notice can be technically correct and still read as a recommendation. They are reviewed against the visible texts, the accessible names, the empty states and the expected-count sort notice (scenario M3).

## 10. Not in this vertical

Any prediction, probability, composite score or quality indicator of the ordering; any use of outcomes after 2025 or of any study origin; the study's null model; publication beyond the tailnet; the OEPM consultation (P7); and the validation of anything.
