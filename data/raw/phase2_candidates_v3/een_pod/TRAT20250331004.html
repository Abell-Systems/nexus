General information

Short Summary
An Austrian company with longstanding experience in the field of process optimisation for wastewater and anaerobic technologies is looking for partners with innovative solutions and ideas to improve wastewater treatment plants. It is interested in optimising the use of polymer flocculants and thereby reducing the water content in digested sludge.
The company is looking for technical cooperations focused on the biological or chemical treatment stages of wastewater treatment plants.
Full Description
COMPANY DESCRIPTION
The company’s objective is to enhance the efficiency of municipal and industrial wastewater treatment plants. Hence it is confronted with various issues related to biological and chemical processes regarding the treatment of wastewater.

PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
The sedimentation of small suspended or colloidal solid particles (especially organic substances) is an essential process in various stages of wastewater treatment. These particles are bound together to form larger 'flocs'. These 'flocs' settle much faster due to their mass, which makes the process more efficient. Artificial polymer flocculants are used to create these 'flocs'.

The consumption of flocculants during the dewatering of digested sludge often varies significantly, as digested sludge contains large amounts of capillary and cell water. Some causes are well-known for this phenomenon, such as the fact that certain microorganisms die during the digestion process, releasing cell wall residues that form colloids, which also bind water, or the re-dissolution of phosphates, which can also bind significant amounts of water. As a result, mechanical dewatering methods (e.g. centrifuges, diverse presses) reach their limitations in effectively processing the sludge.

Despite the use of flocculants, a considerable proportion of bound water remains in the sludge, which limits the achievable dry matter content. Precise process control, optimised flocculant dosing, and potentially pre-treatment measures such as the use of dewatering aids or sludge conditioning can help mitigate these challenges.

Therefore, the amount of flocculants used should be minimised for cost reasons without negatively influencing the sedimentation process. In fact, a suitable flocculation process ensures that the proportion of bound water can be minimised and the achievable dry matter content can be optimised. This is of interest as a high water content increases the costs for further treatment of the digested sludge (e.g. incineration).

STATE OF THE ART
Currently, many of the factors limiting the effectiveness of flocculation processes remain unknown. Flocculants do not always work equally well, even though they are used in the same wastewater treatment plant. In addition to the well-known charge changes between summer and winter sludge, there are also significant fluctuations in polymer consumption that occur independently of seasonal variations. The reasons for this are not yet sufficiently understood. Whenever flocculant consumption increases or the dry matter content of the dewatered sludge decreases, immediate action is required.

In practice, fresh samples of digested sludge are taken from the wastewater treatment plants and flocculation trials are conducted in the laboratory. The flocculants identified during these trials are subsequently tested in pilot-scale experiments and then used in the wastewater treatment plant for as long as the performance meets the expectations of the plant operator. Once the efficiency of the process decreases, new samples are collected and additional tests are carried out to identify the appropriate flocculants. These tests are both time-consuming and cost-intensive for flocculant suppliers and plant operators alike.

The goal is to understand the causes of fluctuations, improve the efficiency of sludge dewatering, reduce resource consumption, and simultaneously minimise operating costs and time required for trials and adjustments. The emphasis should remain on fostering sustainable and innovative development throughout the process.

PARTNER SOUGHT
The Austrian company is interested in a commercial agreement with technical assistance as well as in joint development projects.
Technical Specification or Expertise Sought
The company is seeking expertise in process optimisation and associated technologies (e.g. sensors, equipment) that enable the cost-efficient and effective use of flocculants in municipal and industrial wastewater treatment plants. Suppliers of novel and innovative flocculants are also welcome.

Particular interest lies in solutions and expertise that contribute to reducing the water content of digested sludge and improving sludge dewatering performance.

Expertise in the following areas is particularly desirable:
- causes of fluctuations occurring with flocculants
- process control
- optimised flocculant dosing
- pre-treatment measures such as the use of dewatering aids or sludge conditioning
- efficiency of sludge dewatering
- general reduction in resource consumption
- …

Partner Sought

Expected Role of a Partner
The Austrian company is seeking collaboration with companies and research institutions that possess innovative technologies and expertise in the biological and chemical treatment of municipal and industrial wastewater. Particular interest lies in solutions that improve process efficiency, reduce the water content of digested sludge, and minimise the consumption of polymer flocculants.
Organisations offering novel approaches, technologies, equipment, monitoring systems, or specialist knowledge in these fields are invited to cooperate.
Potential partnerships may take the form of joint development projects, licensing agreements, technical cooperation agreements, or other mutually beneficial forms of collaboration.
