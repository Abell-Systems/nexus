General information

Short Summary
A Greek university is preparing an Erasmus+ Centres of Vocational Excellence proposal focused on vocational skills for digital water, environmental monitoring and blue-economy applications. Partners are sought mainly from Bulgaria, Romania, Austria and Serbia, especially VET providers and universities, together with technology, operational and public-sector actors connected to the Danube–Black Sea area.
Full Description
A Greek university is preparing an Erasmus+ proposal under the call ERASMUS-EDU-2026-PEX-COVE, aiming to strengthen vocational skills for the use of digital water and ocean technologies in the Danube River–Delta–Black Sea area. The project will focus on making advanced tools such as earth observation, geographic information systems, artificial intelligence, big data and digital twin approaches easier to understand and use in vocational education, work-based learning and operational environments.

The proposal addresses the gap between fast-developing environmental and blue-economy technologies and the practical skills available among learners, teachers, trainers, workers, SMEs, ports, waterway operators, environmental actors and public authorities. The objective is to create training pathways and practical learning activities that help these groups apply digital tools to real water, river, coastal, port and marine challenges.

The emerging consortium is currently built around Greek expertise in higher education, geospatial and environmental applications, and project development. It is now seeking complementary partners from Danube-region countries in order to complete the transnational partnership. More specifically, partners are sought from countries connected to the Danube–Black Sea area, with priority given to Bulgaria, Romania, Austria and Serbia. The main need is for vocational education and training providers and universities with expertise in water management, environmental sciences, geoinformatics and blue economy. Additional partners are sought from the technology/data field, operational actors such as ports, waterway operators and environmental organisations, as well as public-sector bodies able to support regional uptake and long-term use of the project results.

The selected partners will join the Erasmus+ proposal consortium and contribute to the preparation and implementation of the project under a research and development cooperation agreement.
Advantages and Innovations
The project’s added value lies in translating advanced digital water and blue-economy technologies into practical vocational skills. Instead of focusing only on research or technology development, the proposal aims to make these tools teachable, usable and relevant for learners, trainers, workers and organisations active in river, delta, coastal and marine environments.

The approach is innovative because it connects education providers with technology experts, operational actors and public authorities around real environmental and blue-economy challenges. This can help improve vocational training, support the green and digital transition, and create stronger links between education, labour-market needs and regional development in the Danube–Black Sea area.
Technical Specification or Expertise Sought
The consortium is looking for partners from Bulgaria, Romania, Austria and Serbia, with a strong preference for organisations connected to the Danube River, Danube Delta, Black Sea, inland waterways, coastal areas, water management or blue economy.

The main partners sought are:

Vocational education and training providers and adult learning providers: organisations able to develop, adapt and test vocational training activities for learners, teachers, trainers or workers in fields such as water management, environmental monitoring, geoinformatics, blue economy, ports, logistics, sustainability or digital skills.

Universities and applied research organisations: partners able to provide scientific and methodological input on digital water and environmental applications, including earth observation, geographic information systems, artificial intelligence, big data, environmental monitoring, coastal/marine systems or blue-economy innovation.

Technology and data actors: SMEs, research institutes or other organisations working with earth observation, geographic information systems, artificial intelligence, environmental data, water data, digital tools or decision-support systems. Their role will be to help translate technical tools into practical training examples and learning tasks.

Operational actors: ports, waterway operators, utilities, SMEs, environmental organisations or protected-area managers that can provide real operational challenges, pilot cases and feedback on the usefulness of the skills and training activities for workplace needs.

Public-sector and uptake actors: municipalities, regional authorities, qualification bodies or sector organisations that can support stakeholder engagement, policy uptake, skills recognition, dissemination and long-term use of the project results.

Partner Sought

Expected Role of a Partner
VET providers and adult learning providers will support the design and testing of vocational training content, learning activities and work-based learning approaches. They are expected to involve learners, teachers, trainers or adult learners and provide feedback on how the training can be integrated into existing education or reskilling pathways.

Universities and research organisations will contribute expertise on the thematic areas of the project, especially digital water technologies, environmental monitoring, earth observation, geographic information systems, artificial intelligence, big data, coastal/marine systems and blue-economy applications. They may also support the scientific quality of training material and applied case studies.

Technology and data partners will help convert technical tools and datasets into practical examples that can be used by non-expert learners and workers. They may contribute to demonstrations, simplified workflows, training datasets, dashboards or applied learning scenarios.

Operational partners will provide real-life challenges from ports, waterways, utilities, protected areas, environmental management or blue-economy SMEs. Their role will be to validate whether the proposed skills and training activities are relevant for workplace needs and to support work-based learning pilots.

Municipalities, regional authorities, qualification bodies and sector organisations will support local and regional engagement, dissemination, policy alignment, skills recognition and long-term adoption of the project outcomes.
