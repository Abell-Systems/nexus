General information

Short Summary
A Dutch university department of family medicine and public health research is seeking to join one or more Horizon Europe consortium on prevention and management of chronic NCDs in children and young people. It wishes to join as a project partner or work package leader. They could contribute to topics such as primary care implementation science, behavioural prevention, tobacco/nicotine cessation, and real-world evaluation in both high-income and low- and middle- income settings.
Full Description
This university department of family medicine and public health research in the Netherlands, active in preventive intervention research, implementation science, and behavioural health in primary care, is seeking to join one or more existing or forming consortium under the Horizon Europe topic HORIZON-HLTH-2027-01-DISEASE-10: Prevention and management of chronic non-communicable diseases in children and young people (GACD), as a project partner or, depending on the consortium's needs, as work package leader — particularly for work packages on intervention development, implementation strategy, process evaluation, stakeholder engagement, real-world evaluation, or dissemination.

The project it wishes to contribute to addresses the rising burden of chronic non-communicable diseases (NCDs) among children and young people, such as those linked to tobacco and nicotine use and unhealthy lifestyle behaviours. The call seeks to develop, adapt, and scale up evidence-based prevention and management strategies that reach the young people who need them most, including in under-resourced and underserved communities, and to demonstrate that these strategies remain effective and sustainable beyond the life of the project.

The organisation brings two complementary strands of expertise.

First, broader primary-care implementation research: developing, implementing and evaluating preventive interventions in routine primary care in both European and low- and middle-income settings (e.g. Ethiopia), including digital health innovations, self-management support for chronic conditions, and pragmatic or platform trial methods.

Second, a specific focus on tobacco and nicotine prevention and cessation among adolescents and young adults, including behavioural counselling, digital and AI-supported support tools, financial incentives, and policy-level approaches, alongside strong methodological expertise in evaluation design (including randomised and quasi-experimental studies) and equitable implementation among disadvantaged groups.
Technical Specification or Expertise Sought
Looking for an existing or forming Horizon Europe consortium under this topic, which the organisation could join as project partner, work package leader, implementation/evaluation partner, or pilot/data partner.

Looking for a consortium coordinator or partners developing prevention and management strategies for NCDs in children and young people, particularly around tobacco/nicotine, lifestyle-related risk factors, behavioural prevention, digital health support, or equitable implementation for underserved populations. Ideally, seeking within the primary care implementation research domain or behavioural/health-economic evaluation.

Partner Sought

Expected Role of a Partner
As a partner within the consortium, the department could take on responsibility for intervention development, implementation strategy, and process or outcome evaluation for NCD prevention programmes targeting children and young people. It is prepared to lead or substantially contribute to a work package on implementation science and stakeholder engagement, covering adaptation and tailoring of evidence-based interventions, measurement of implementation and real-world effectiveness outcomes, and equitable-implementation strategies to ensure interventions reach the populations that need them most.

Specific tasks it could perform include: designing and evaluating behavioural and digital prevention interventions, including AI-supported cessation support for tobacco and nicotine use among adolescents and young adults; running quantitative and qualitative evaluations (e.g., randomised and quasi-experimental designs, process evaluation, survey and interview-based research); co-creating interventions with young people, healthcare professionals, schools, employers, and community organisations; and generating evidence directly relevant to policymakers, communities and practitioners, including a strategy for sustaining the intervention beyond the project's lifetime.

It would also bring supporting infrastructure and data assets, including access to Dutch primary care electronic health record data (e.g., the Research Network Family Medicine in Limburg), international public health and clinical data from long-standing partnerships in Ethiopia (maternal and child health, HIV, tuberculosis and chronic disease programmes), implementation-mapping and process-evaluation tools, and networks spanning general practices, schools, community organisations, smoking cessation services, and public health authorities in both the Netherlands and low- and middle-income partner countries. It also holds a board position in the Society for Research on Nicotine and Tobacco Europe (SRNT-E).

Relevant track record includes an international implementation project on tobacco/nicotine prevention and clean air among disadvantaged young people across Uganda, Romania, Pakistan, Greece and Kyrgyzstan (HORIZON-HLTH-2022-DISEASE-07, FRESHAIR4Life); development of a blended vaping-cessation intervention for 16–25-year-olds; workplace smoking-cessation programmes with financial incentives for lower socioeconomic groups; tobacco policy and cessation-campaign evaluations; and implementation research on maternal, child and preventive health interventions in Ethiopian primary care.

The exact scope of tasks and deliverables would need to be agreed jointly with the coordinating consortium, depending on the work package structure and overall project needs.
